# Player City Revamp


## Summary

Player cities get four upgrades: elections that decide policy, work for city officials between elections, NPCs and stores that make cities feel lived in, and benefits that exist only inside a city.

- **Edicts and platforms.** Candidates run on Edicts, city-wide policies the treasury pays for. The winner's Edicts take effect, so a vote changes rewards, prices, and who is welcome.
- **City Affairs.** The mayor and appointed officers handle situations at City Hall, such as strikes, petitions, bribes, and beast sightings. Each decision has a small real effect and goes in the City Gazette for everyone to roleplay over.
- **Living cities.** Building staff, market stalls, and travelers who move to a new city each week sell items found nowhere else, and a sales tax pays the treasury. Hostile spawns keep clear of buildings.
- **Conflict.** A Dueling Ring, city tournaments, and city bounties on players who mark themselves open to PvP give PvP a venue and a story, with no combat bonuses.
- **City-only benefits.** Workshops, farming, the Civic Exchange, flights home, and Home Ground exist only for cities and their citizens. After the space revamp, planetary industry joins them.

Goals: votes decide something citizens feel, holding office is worth it, and cities differ from each other and generate stories. Everything here applies to the space revamp's player stations too. Defense events and mayor perks are later plans.

A player can have characters in different cities. Each character gets only its own city's bonuses.

**How bonuses stack**

- Festival Season adds 10% of the RP XP award's 1,500 base, or 150 XP, the same base the Cantina's 5% per level uses. A festival situation adds 5% of it. They add together.
- Hunting Grounds adds 10 points to the skill XP bonus that Social, status effects, and DM bonuses already add to, for XP from defeating enemies only. Crafting, harvesting, farming, fishing, and Leadership XP are not included.
- Artisans' Charter adds 10 points to that same bonus for crafting XP at stations in the city, so it never overlaps Hunting Grounds.
- Guild Charter adds 10% to a guild task's GP and credits. Drydock Subsidy adds 15 points to the Starport's repair discount, still capped at 90%.
- Each bonus is checked when it pays out: XP and guild rewards when they are awarded, crafting when the craft finishes, harvests when the item is gathered, and breeding when a stage starts.

**Harvesting bonuses**

Harvesting already gives a second item on a d100 roll of up to 25 per Might modifier, so it is certain from Might 18 (modifier +4). Adding points to that roll would do nothing for established gatherers. Prospectors' Charter and Home Ground are separate rolls instead: a 10% and a 5% chance of 1 more item from the same node, without using up the node. The Might roll is unchanged.

| Might modifier | Items per harvest today | With Prospectors' Charter | With the Charter and Home Ground |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 1.00 | 1.10 (+10%) | 1.15 (+15%) |
| +1 | 1.25 | 1.35 (+8%) | 1.40 (+12%) |
| +2 | 1.50 | 1.60 (+7%) | 1.65 (+10%) |
| +3 | 1.75 | 1.85 (+6%) | 1.90 (+9%) |
| +4 or more | 2.00 | 2.10 (+5%) | 2.15 (+8%) |

The two rolls are independent, so a harvest can give up to 2 more items. On average they add 0.15 items per harvest, so a fully skilled gatherer gets about 8% more.

Why elections are ignored:

- **Being mayor means paying the bills.** The office brings no personal benefit.
- **The incumbent usually wins by default.** They keep the seat on a tie, with no votes, or with no candidates.
- **Candidates have nothing to offer.** The election window lists names only.
- **Elections happen off-screen.** Notices go to Discord only, and nothing in game announces them.
- **Voting is rarely open.** It runs 7 days out of every 42.
- **Every city offers the same small percentages,** so there is little to choose between cities.

## Player experience

Every role gets a decision to make or a reason to show up.

- **Candidate.** Registers at City Hall, picks Edicts up to the city's slot count, and writes a short statement. Then campaigns in character: speeches, posters, cantina rounds, endorsements.
- **Citizen voter.** Sees an in-game notice while registration and voting are open. Compares candidates' Edicts, costs, statements, and the incumbent's record, then votes knowing the result changes their rewards, prices, and who is welcome.
- **Mayor.** Takes office with their platform in force and sets taxes to pay for it. Appoints officers, handles City Affairs, and answers for both at the next election.
- **Officer.** Holds a title the mayor gives, like Minister of Trade or Marshal. Handles City Affairs at City Hall and earns Leadership XP for it. Every decision goes in the Gazette under their name.
- **Visitor.** Notices that cities differ. One welcomes outsiders through Open Gates, another hosts a dueling tournament, a third pays its crafters more. Reading a city's Gazette tells them what kind of place it is.

Example: a Village (city level 2, one Edict slot). Candidate A runs on Guild Charter and low taxes. Candidate B runs on Open Gates and wants a busy trade town. Last week the mayor took a smuggler's bribe, and the Gazette exposed it. The vote settles what kind of town it becomes next.

## Edict rules

A city runs one to three Edicts, depending on its level. Each adds a weekly cost to upkeep and pauses while the city is in disrepair.

| City level | Edict slots |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Outpost, 2 Village | 1 |
| 3 Township, 4 City | 2 |
| 5 Metropolis | 3 |

1. **Cost.** Enacting an Edict mid-term pays for the days left until the next weekly upkeep check at once, from the treasury, so it can't be enacted without the funds. After that, each weekly check (`ProcessUpkeep`) adds a full week to the upkeep bill for every Edict that isn't paused, beside facility costs. An Edict paused by a situation outcome is still billed, since that pause is the outcome.
2. **Who benefits.** See Who qualifies under Bonuses at a glance.
3. **Enactment.** When results post, an outright winner's platform replaces every active Edict, whatever their locks, and its first part-week is added to the upkeep bill. An incumbent kept without an outright win keeps the current Edicts (see the outcome tables under Election changes).
4. **Lock and repeal.** Every Edict, whether from a platform or enacted mid-term, is locked for 7 days after it takes effect. After that the mayor can repeal it from Manage City. Repealing refunds nothing, so switching an Edict on and off still pays for every day it ran.
5. **Broken promises.** Repealing an Edict from the winning platform marks it "Promise broken" on the city's public record. Citizens see that record in the Citizenship window and the next election window. An Edict paused by the city's level is not a broken promise.
6. **Upkeep bills and disrepair.** The weekly check adds the week's bill, and the mayor pays it from the treasury, as today. The city is in disrepair only when a bill is still unpaid at the next weekly check, so Edicts keep working while a fresh bill waits to be paid.
7. **City-wide pauses.** Three conditions belong to the city, not to any one Edict, and pause every Edict while they hold, including one enacted later:
   - Disrepair: from the weekly check that finds a bill unpaid until that bill is paid.
   - No confidence: from a failed confidence vote until an election ends with an outright winner.
   - Neglect: from an empty second recall until an election with at least one candidate and one vote ends.
8. **Single-Edict pauses.** An Edict also pauses when the city's level drops below its minimum or its slot is lost (until the level returns), or for a situation outcome such as gates closed for 72 hours (until it expires). An Edict runs only when no city-wide or single-Edict pause covers it.
9. **No enacting during a city-wide pause.** The mayor can't enact a new Edict while the city is in disrepair, under No confidence, or under Neglect.
10. **Resuming between checks.** An Edict that resumes between weekly checks adds its part-week to the upkeep bill, because the last check skipped it. One that resumes at a weekly check is billed there as usual.
11. **Exclusive pairs.** A city cannot hold an Alliance and an Embargo against the same city. A pending Alliance, waiting for the other mayor, takes a slot but costs nothing until both cities enact it, and its lock starts then.
12. **Permission.** A new Manage Edicts city permission goes to the mayor, who alone can grant it (see Permissions under City Affairs).

**When the city level changes**

The level changes only at the weekly upkeep check, at the first server start after it is due (`ProcessCityLevel`), as today, and this plan keeps that timing. It drops at once to what the mayor's City Management rank and the citizen count support, and rises one level per check while the last bill is paid.

| Feature | When the level drops | When it returns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Edicts | Those whose minimum level is now too high pause first, then the newest above the new slot count | They resume |
| A new mayor's platform | Enacted in full when results post, then trimmed at the next check. Paused Edicts are not broken promises | They resume |
| Officers | The most recent appointments above the new limit end | The mayor can appoint again |
| Market stalls | The newest stalls above the limit close, and the structures stay | They reopen |
| Corrals | No new animals above the new limit of 2 corrals per level; animals already there stay | Normal |
| Townsfolk, guards, and the Settled zone | Follow the current level | Follow the current level |
| Facility bonuses | Work at the lower of the facility level and the city level, as today | As today |

## Edict catalog

The set has twelve Edicts across RP, diplomacy, economy, PvE, crafting, and industry. PvP gets places and reasons to fight instead (see Conflict and PvP). Every effect size and cost is a proposal until approved. Facility upgrades, at 10,000 a week for each level above 1, were the yardstick.

| Edict | Pillar | Effect | Cost / week | Min level | Workstream |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Festival Season | RP | Citizens earn 150 more RP XP per award (10% of its 1,500 base), on top of the Cantina | 10,000 | 1 | 2 |
| Alliance | Diplomacy | Names one city. When both mayors enact it, each city's citizens can use the other's Bank and Starport | 5,000 | 1 | 2 |
| Embargo | Diplomacy | Names one city. Its citizens are barred from this city's facilities, even under Open Gates | 2,500 | 1 | 2 |
| Open Gates | Economy | Non-citizens can use the Bank and Starport. Visitors pay the transportation tax into the treasury | 5,000 | 1 | 2 |
| Guild Charter | PvE | Citizens earn +10% GP and credits from guild tasks | 15,000 | 1 | 2 |
| Prospectors' Charter | PvE | Citizens harvesting on the city's planet get a separate 10% chance of 1 more item per harvest | 10,000 | 1 | 2 |
| Drydock Subsidy | PvE | Citizens pay 15% less for ship repairs, on top of the Starport. The 90% cap stays | 10,000 | 1 | 2 |
| Hunting Grounds | PvE | Citizens earn +10% XP from defeating enemies on the city's planet | 15,000 | 3 | 2 |
| Artisans' Charter | Crafting | Citizens earn +10% crafting XP at stations inside the city, including houses built there | 10,000 | 1 | 2 |
| Breeders' Charter | Crafting | Citizens' incubation stages in the city's Labs raise each purity stat 10% more, even past the 10% stage cap (6.0% becomes 6.6%) | 10,000 | 1 | 2 |
| Industrial Charter | Industry | Citizens' holdings on the city's planet roll setbacks half as often and need half the repair goods | 5,000 | 3 | 7 |
| Shadowport | Economy | The Starport exchange buys contraband at close to black-market prices, with a small chance per sale of a customs bust (lower with a Smuggling Compartment). Normal goods sell 15% lower | 10,000 | 1 | 7 |

- **Artisans' and Breeders' Charter** apply only inside the city, including houses built there, which makes owning one worth more. Breeders' Charter adds a tenth of what a stage's enzymes add to each purity stat they raised (Attack, Accuracy, Evasion, Learning, the two defenses, and the eight resistances, never mutation chance or the XP penalty), rounded down to 0.1%, after the 10% stage cap and before the 100% total cap. Example: a stage whose three enzymes add 7.3% Attack, 1.5% Accuracy, and 0.8% Evasion gets 0.7%, 0.1%, and nothing more; a stage that would add 12.0% Attack is capped at 10.0% and becomes 11.0%. It adds purity rather than speed because a shorter 36-hour stage still ends overnight for most players, and a share of the gain rather than a flat point keeps weak enzymes from gaining the most.
- **Hunting Grounds and Prospectors' Charter** pull citizens to adventure near home, so the city's planet sees more players and more chance meetings. Planet-based effects apply in the planet's ground areas, not in space.
- **Open Gates, Alliance, and Embargo** turn facility access into a diplomatic tool that mayors can negotiate with.
- **Without Shadowport,** a city's exchange is a lawful port: it still buys contraband, but at low prices and with a high bust chance. A bust means a fine, the cargo confiscated, and lawful standing lost, as the space revamp's customs rules define.

**Who may use the Bank and Starport**, checked in this order:

1. Citizens of a city under this city's Embargo: refused.
2. Citizens of this city: allowed if they owe no taxes, as today. A citizen who owes taxes is refused, even under Open Gates, until they pay at City Hall.
3. Citizens of an allied city with no taxes owed at home: allowed.
4. Anyone else while Open Gates is active: allowed, paying the transportation tax on flights.
5. Everyone else: refused, as today.

- The check happens at the Bank and Starport doors. Anyone inside when access changes keeps using the building until they leave, and city land outside the buildings never closes, so anyone can walk out of a planet city.
- Departure access is separate from facility access. A player whose ship is docked at the Starport, or who landed there on a Home Port flight, can enter the Starport for 7 days to board that ship or take a flight out, and nothing else: no Bank, no exchange, no docking another ship.
- After the 7 days, a ship still docked there is moved to the planet's public starport, or to a station's home port, the way the space revamp already moves a ship whose station Starport is removed. Its owner collects it there.
- On a station, the Starport's flights terminal admits everyone for departure, Embargo included, because it is the only way off for a player without a ship.
- Shops, travelers, and the Gazette are outside these rules, and house permissions are unchanged.

| Facility upkeep, 10,000 per level above 1 | 0 | 30,000 | 120,000 |
| Edicts | Festival Season: 10,000 | Artisans' and Prospectors' Charter: 20,000 | Hunting Grounds, Guild Charter, and Festival Season: 40,000 |
| Weekly cost | 45,000 | 85,000 | 195,000 |
| Citizenship tax per citizen to cover it alone | 4,500 | 5,700 | 7,800 |

- Edicts are a fifth to a quarter of a city's weekly cost, so a platform is a real choice without crowding out facilities, which stay the largest cost for a full city.
- City Affairs covers at most about one Edict a week, can't be withdrawn, and every gain carries a cost, so profitable choices can't fund a city on their own.
- Sales and transportation taxes reward busy cities, and the 10% Sales tax cap keeps shoppers coming.

**Money held for prizes, bounties, and projects**

- When posted, bounty rewards, tournament prizes and entry fees, and a City Project's credits move into escrow. Nobody can spend or withdraw them, and a mayor change doesn't touch them.
- Bounties and prizes come from the ordinary treasury. A City Project spends restricted funds first, and escrow records how much came from each balance.
- Paid out: a bounty to its claimant, a prize to the winner when the final ends, and a project's credits when it completes.
- Returned: a refund puts each part back in the balance it came from, so cancelling a project can't turn restricted situation income into credits the mayor can withdraw. Unclaimed or cancelled bounties and cancelled prizes go back to the treasury, and a cancelled tournament's entry fees go back to the entrants.
- City Projects: a donation is taken only up to what the project still needs. A project stays open until supplied or cancelled. If it is cancelled, donated materials go to each donor's overflow store, which they claim with /overflow, as items that didn't fit after a character upgrade already are.
- A removed city: entry fees are paid back to the entrants at their next login, and everything else goes with the treasury, as today.
- Project materials for each facility level are drafted with the stock lists and approved before release.

## Election changes

Candidates register a platform and a statement, citizens see the election in game, and the quiet stretch between elections gets shorter.

1. **Platform at registration.** Enter Race opens a platform step. The candidate picks Edicts up to the city's slot count, respecting level minimums, and writes a statement of up to 600 characters (the HoloNet limit). Both can be edited until registration closes. Running with no Edicts is allowed.
2. **Cost preview.** The platform step shows the platform's weekly cost next to current upkeep, treasury, and weekly tax income. Candidates see what their promises will cost.
3. **City Management warning.** Anyone can run today, but a mayor's City Management rank caps the city level (`ProcessCityLevel`). The platform step warns when the candidate's rank would lower the city level and its Edict slots.
4. **Election window.** Each candidate row shows name, Edicts, weekly cost, and statement. The incumbent's row adds their record, such as "Promises kept: 2 of 3". Today the window lists names only.
5. **In-game notices.** City processing runs only at server start, so notices are delivered at login. Citizens see one at each login while registration or voting is open, with the days left, and once after results post. The Discord webhook stays as it is.
6. **Confidence vote.** A lone candidate no longer wins outright. Citizens vote Yes or No for 7 days. Every outcome is in the tables below.
7. **Shorter calendar.** Registration drops from 14 days to 7 and the quiet period from 21 to 14. Voting stays at 7. A contested cycle becomes 28 days instead of 42.
8. **Voter reward.** Casting a vote grants 10 RP points, once per CD key every 28 days across all cities. The vote is already one per CD key, so the reward cannot be farmed with alts.

**Election outcomes**

Every election runs 7 days of registration and 7 days of voting, then 14 quiet days before the next regular election opens. A regular election follows that calendar. A recall opens after neglect (see Neglect and recall). A snap election opens after a failed confidence vote or when the mayor steps down. A recall or snap election takes the next regular election's place, and the 14 quiet days start when it ends.

When registration closes:

| Candidates | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| None | No vote. The incumbent stays (in a step-down snap election, the interim holder becomes mayor). A recall with no candidates counts as empty |
| One | A 7-day confidence vote, Yes or No |
| Two or more | A 7-day contested vote |

When voting closes:

| Result | Regular or recall election | Snap election |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yes wins a confidence vote | An outright win: the candidate takes office, or the incumbent is confirmed, and their platform is enacted | The same |
| No wins against a challenger | The incumbent stays with the current Edicts | The incumbent stays, with Edicts still paused if confidence was lost |
| No wins against the incumbent | No confidence: Edicts pause and a snap election opens at once. The incumbent may run in it | The incumbent stays with Edicts paused until the next regular election. A snap election never opens another |
| Yes and No tie | The incumbent stays with the current Edicts | The incumbent stays, with Edicts still paused if confidence was lost |
| One candidate has the most votes | An outright win: they take office, and their platform is enacted | The same |
| Tie for most votes | The incumbent stays with the current Edicts, even if they were one of the tied candidates | The incumbent stays, with Edicts still paused if confidence was lost |
| No votes, or every candidate left | The incumbent stays with the current Edicts. A recall that ends this way is empty | The incumbent stays, with Edicts still paused if confidence was lost |

- Only an outright win, a Yes or the most votes, enacts a platform and ends a No confidence pause. Ties, No results, and elections without votes leave things as they were.
- Any election that ends with at least one candidate and one vote ends a Neglect pause, whatever its result, because citizens took part.
- Pauses belong to the city, so a winner's new Edicts are paused too while a pause the election didn't end still holds.
- In a step-down snap election, "the incumbent" means the interim holder, who becomes mayor wherever the incumbent would stay. The mayor who stepped down cannot run.
- Candidates register, edit their platform, or withdraw until registration closes. Then the ballot is fixed.
- A candidate who leaves the city or is deleted during voting is removed. Votes for them are dropped, and those voters can vote again. If every candidate leaves, the election ends as if nobody had voted.
- A character can vote if it is a citizen and has been one since before registration opened. Leaving the city or deleting the character drops its vote, and rejoining doesn't restore it for that election.
- One vote per CD key in each city's election, as today, so a player with characters in several cities can vote in each. The voter reward goes to the character that casts its CD key's vote, once per CD key every 28 days across all cities. Changing or withdrawing a vote never pays it again.

## City Affairs

Between elections, the mayor and officers handle situations at a City Affairs terminal in City Hall. Each situation has two or three choices with small, real effects, and every decision is published in the City Gazette.

**How it works**

1. **Officers.** The mayor appoints officers and gives each a title. Officers get the Handle City Affairs permission. A city can have 1 officer plus 1 per city level, so 2 to 6.
2. **Situations arrive.** At each server start, a city with fewer than 3 open situations, and none drawn in the last 24 hours, draws one. Situations fit the city's level, planet, facilities, and active Edicts.
3. **Deciding.** The mayor or an officer chooses at the terminal, in person. Some choices need a Leadership or Social rank and give skilled officials a better outcome.
4. **Effects stay small.** A treasury cost of up to 15,000 credits or a gain of up to 10,000, a modifier lasting 72 hours, a follow-up situation, or a story hook. Nothing permanent: no situation destroys a crop, an animal, or a structure.
5. **Ignoring has a cost.** A situation left for 72 hours takes its ignored outcome, usually the worst one, and the Gazette says the city ignored it. An ignored outcome never takes more than the treasury holds.
6. **Reward.** The official who decides earns Leadership XP. Situations are limited per city, so this cannot be farmed.
7. **Secrets.** Some choices, like taking a bribe, stay out of the Gazette at first. A later situation can expose them.

**Limits that keep it casual**

- Gains: situation gains count toward a cap of 20,000 credits a week per city and go to the treasury's restricted funds, which pay upkeep, Edicts, and City Projects but cannot be withdrawn. Every choice that gains credits also carries a cost: a 72-hour penalty, a chance of exposure, or a worse follow-up. Taking every profitable choice can't fund a city, and nobody profits personally from holding office in several cities.
- Repeats: only one situation of each type is open at a time, and a repeated modifier restarts its 72 hours instead of stacking.
- Timing: timers run in real time. A situation that expires while the server is down resolves at the next server start. Follow-ups arrive at the first server start after they are due, once fewer than 3 situations are open.
- Workload: at most one new situation a day and three open at once, so seven decisions a week at most. Deciding one in every five keeps the city clear of a recall, and ignoring the rest costs only their listed outcomes.

**Example situations**

| Situation | Choices and effects |
| --- | --- |
| Dock workers strike | Pay the raise: -15,000. Refuse: transportation tax income halved for 72 hours. Negotiate (Leadership 10): -7,500. |
| Festival petition | Fund it: -10,000, and citizens earn +5% RP XP for 72 hours. Decline: a Gazette entry. |
| Smuggler's offer | Take the bribe: +10,000 to restricted funds, kept secret, with a 1 in 3 chance a later Scandal situation exposes it. Arrest the smuggler: a story hook for DMs. |
| Beast sighting near the city | Post a bounty: -5,000 and a story hook. Ignore it: a Caravan Attacked situation follows in 3 days. |
| Refugee shuttle | Welcome them: -10,000, follow-up New Arrivals situations, and a story hook. Turn them away: a Gazette entry. |
| Medical supply shortage | Buy supplies: -10,000. Ration: the Medical Center bonus is halved for 72 hours. |
| Visitor brawl (needs Open Gates) | Fine the visitors: +5,000, and a Visitors Complain follow-up. Close the gates: Open Gates is suspended for 72 hours. |
| Pests in the gardens | Hire exterminators: -5,000. Ignore it: crops harvested in the city in the next 72 hours yield 1 less, never below 1. Crops keep growing. |
| Predators at the corrals | Call a hunt: an NPC attack at the corrals for 30 minutes, which starts only when an official chooses it, and the animals cannot be harmed. Ignore it, or lose the hunt: the city's animals give no products for 72 hours. Animal owners get a login notice either way. |

**City Gazette**

- A public log for each city: decisions with the official's name and title, Edicts enacted and repealed, tax changes, and election results.
- Anyone can read it at a Gazette terminal in City Hall, visitors included. Citizens can also read it in the Citizenship window, and entries post to the city Discord feed.
- The election window shows the incumbent's recent decisions next to their promises.

**Story hooks**

- Some outcomes, like an ignored beast sighting, a welcomed refugee ship, or an exposed scandal, also go to a DM feed. DMs can run events from them, so a city's choices reach the wider story.
- Staff and players can write new situations. Each is a short entry with its text, choices, effects, and conditions. Player-written situations go through staff review.
- The mayor or an officer can post one open story request at City Hall, up to 600 characters, such as an invitation for a smuggling plot or a hint about ruins near the city. It goes to the DM feed, and a new request replaces the old one.
- A DM city window lists every city with its mayor and officers, how many citizens logged in during the last week, its active Edicts, recent Gazette entries, and its open story request. DMs can see which cities are lived in and what stories they want, and mark a request taken.

**Acting mayor and stepping down**

- The mayor can name a citizen Acting Mayor for up to 30 days, for example while away from the game, and the Gazette announces it. It ends when the mayor ends it, after 30 days, when the Acting Mayor leaves the city, or when an election seats a mayor. Anything they did stays done.
- The city's level keeps following the mayor's City Management rank, and situations the Acting Mayor handles count toward the neglect rule.
- A mayor can also step down for good by naming an interim holder: their Acting Mayor or one of their officers. The named player accepts in Manage City within 7 days, or the step-down is cancelled. A mayor with neither must name an Acting Mayor first. A snap election then opens at once.
- Until the snap election ends, the interim holder has the Acting Mayor's powers, and the departed mayor stays mayor of record with no powers, so the city's level still follows their rank. City permissions they granted stay as they are until then.

**Permissions**

| Power | Mayor | Acting Mayor or interim holder | Officer | Holder of a granted permission |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Appoint officers or an Acting Mayor | Yes | No | No | No |
| Grant city permissions | Yes | No | No | Only permissions they hold with grant rights, as today, never Manage Edicts or Handle City Affairs |
| Handle City Affairs | Yes | Yes | Yes | No. Only officers get it, so the officer limit can't be bypassed |
| Enact Edicts into empty slots | Yes | Yes | No | With Manage Edicts, which only the mayor grants |
| Repeal Edicts | Yes | No | No | With Manage Edicts |
| Change taxes | Yes | Yes | No | With Edit Taxes |
| Withdraw from the treasury | Yes | No | No | With Access Treasury |
| Pay upkeep and start City Projects | Yes | Yes | No | With Manage Upkeep or Manage Upgrades |
| Post bounties, tournaments, and story requests | Yes | Yes | Yes | No |

When a different player takes the seat, every officer appointment and every city permission granted under the old mayor ends, and the new mayor grants their own. A re-elected mayor keeps theirs.

**Neglect and recall**

- If 5 situations in a row expire unhandled, a recall election opens at once on the usual calendar, and the sitting mayor may run. Deciding any situation resets the count to 0. While a recall is open, situations still arrive and expire, but they can't open a second recall.
- A recall is empty if no candidate registers, no votes are cast, or every candidate leaves. A recall that isn't empty ends the recall path: its result follows the outcome tables, the count resets to 0, and the regular calendar resumes.
- An empty recall marks the city Abandoned in its Gazette and on Discord, and a second recall opens at once.
- If the second recall is also empty, the game counts the citizens who logged in during the 14 days before it ended. With fewer than 5 (the level 1 minimum), the city is removed the same way as a city that fails upkeep: nobody is using it, and its land should go to someone who will. With 5 or more, citizens use the city but not its politics: it stays, the Abandoned mark clears, its Edicts pause for neglect, and no situations arrive until an election with a candidate and a vote ends.
- Example, where each step happens at the first server start on or after its date. The fifth ignored situation expires on November 2, and a recall opens. Its registration closes November 9 with no candidates, so it is empty: the city is marked Abandoned, and the second recall opens that day. Its registration closes November 16, again with no candidates. The game counts citizens whose last login falls between November 2 and November 16. If 4 did, the city is removed. If 6 did, it stays with its Edicts paused for neglect.

## Living cities

Cities fill with staff, stalls, and travelers whose stores sell items found nowhere else, and a sales tax pays the treasury. Hostile spawns keep clear of buildings.

**Townsfolk**

- **Building staff.** Each city building gets NPCs at fixed spots inside its layout: a clerk in City Hall, a bartender in the Cantina, a medic in the Medical Center, a dockmaster in the Starport, a teller in the Bank. Fixed spots mean placement never depends on how the mayor laid out the city.
- **Market stalls.** The mayor can place Market Stall structures outdoors, up to one per city level. Each stall gets a vendor whose stock theme the mayor picks, such as general goods, crafting supplies, or decor.
- **Ambient townsfolk.** From Village (level 2) up, 2 wandering NPCs per city level walk between the city's buildings, and a few harmless critters roam the streets. From Township (level 3), guards patrol. The mayor can switch townsfolk (wanderers and guards) and critters off separately in Manage City, for a city that wants quiet streets for roleplay. Building staff, stall vendors, and travelers stay at their posts either way.

**Stores and sales tax**

- Staff and stalls sell stock tied to their building, and better facility levels unlock better stock.
- Each store carries a few city-exclusive items, such as Cantina house drinks with a food bonus, and city banners and crests to decorate houses.
- A new Sales tax, set by the mayor from 0% to 10%, is added to each price, rounded down to whole credits, and paid into the treasury. A low tax draws shoppers, a high one funds Edicts. City stores only sell and never buy from players, so there is no buyback or buy-sell loop. Each exclusive item has a weekly allowance that resets at the weekly traveler rotation. Allowances at building staff and stalls belong to the city and the stock theme, and a traveler's to the traveler, so picking up or replacing a stall, changing its theme, a facility level change, or a server restart never refills them.
- Anyone can shop, citizen or not, except citizens of a city under Embargo.

**Travelers**

- A roster of traveling NPCs, each with a story and rotating stock not sold anywhere else: an exotic beast trader, a rare-materials prospector, a fashion merchant, an information broker with rumors and contracts. Names and stories go through lore review.
- Each week, every traveler moves to a different player city. They choose among cities with a Traveler's Lodge, a new structure the mayor places, that haven't hosted them in the last 4 weeks. Cities with Open Gates are twice as likely to be chosen.
- Arrivals are posted in the host city's Gazette, shown to every player at login, and posted to Discord. The host city collects its sales tax on their sales.
- A traveler can bring a City Affairs situation, such as the beast trader asking to keep animals in the plaza.
- If no city qualifies, travelers wait at a fixed public hub, so they are never unreachable.

**Home Port**

- Citizens can book a flight to their city's Starport from any shuttle terminal that flies to the city's planet, not only from the city, and citizens of an allied city can too. Both need no taxes owed and the CZ-220 Shuttle Pass every flight needs. The fare and transportation tax are the same as any flight that terminal sells to that planet, and the tax goes to the city that owns the terminal, if any. Home Port adds an arrival point and lifts no travel limits.
- Starport levels shorten the flight by 30 seconds per level, from 5 minutes down to 2.5 at level 5. It applies once per trip, to the interplanetary leg that starts or ends at the city's Starport. A station's 60-second orbital hop is unchanged.

**Civic Exchange**

- A store in the Cantina where citizens spend Available XP, the RP XP currency the Training Store uses, so roleplay pays off inside the city.
- Stock: crates of random crafting materials, time off a research job running in the city's Labs, cosmetic unlocks such as container and weapon appearances, and a long buff about as strong as a top-tier food buff that does not stack with food. Crates are limited to 3 a week per CD key, each worth under 20 minutes of harvesting at its tier.
- Higher Cantina levels unlock more of the stock.
- Not included: spending RP XP to train a skill faster, because Available XP can already go straight into skills.

**Spawns near buildings**

- Today, random spawns pick any walkable spot in the area, so creatures and resource nodes can appear beside city buildings.
- In a city area, random hostile spawns and resource nodes keep at least 15 m from city buildings, houses, market stalls, corrals, the Dueling Ring, and the Traveler's Lodge. Decorations and garden plots don't count, so cheap structures can't be used to clear the land.
- A Settled zone around City Hall has no hostile spawns at all. Its radius is 10 m per city level, but never more than a quarter of the area's shorter side, which keeps it under about a fifth of any area. 21 of the 29 areas open to cities are 320 m across, where a Metropolis's 50 m zone covers under 8%. The seven smallest, 90 to 160 m across, cap it at 22 to 40 m.
- When no legal spot is left, a random spawn is skipped until its next respawn. Spawns that quests depend on move to the nearest reachable spot outside the zones within 40 m, and otherwise stay where they are today, so no quest breaks.
- Only new spawns are affected. Creatures already there stay until defeated, and wandering or pulled enemies can still walk in: the buffer keeps spawns off buildings, but a city is not a safe zone. Guards are scenery and don't fight.

## Homes, gardens, and projects

Houses get a job, cities get gardens, and big upgrades become group projects, so citizens have reasons to build, visit, and use the city even if they live elsewhere.

**House specializations**

- The owner of a house in a city sets its type: Workshop, Storehouse, or Greenhouse. The type can change once every 30 days. The timer belongs to the house and carries over when the house changes hands.
- **Workshop:** pick one crafting skill. Anyone crafting that skill inside the house gets a Craftsmanship and Control bonus, sized against crafting gear in the balance review. It stacks with Artisans' Charter.
- **Storehouse:** 50% more of the house's own storage, so a small, medium, or large house's 40, 60, or 80 items become 60, 90, or 120. Storage that furniture adds comes on top, unchanged.
- **Greenhouse:** holds twice as many garden plots, and crops there yield 1 more item per harvest.
- Changing type never destroys anything. Leaving Storehouse with more items than the new limit keeps them all, and the storage only allows withdrawals until it is under the limit. Leaving Greenhouse with more than 5 planted plots lets every crop grow to harvest, and plots above 5 can't be replanted.
- A specialization keeps working whoever owns the house, citizen or not, since the house stands in the city.

**Garden plots**

- A new Garden Plot structure, built with Fabrication like other structures, placed only in a city or in a house built there. The plot's look changes as its crop grows.
- Up to 5 plots per house, or 10 in a Greenhouse. Outdoor plots count against the city's structure limit.
- What grows in them, and how, is the Farming section below.

**City Projects** (inspired by No Man's Sky settlements)

- Facility upgrades and Home Ground are built as projects. The treasury pays the credits, and anyone, visitors included, can donate the listed materials at City Hall.
- Once fully supplied, a project takes 2 days of real time to build. The Gazette names the top donors.

## Farming (Agriculture)

Farming completes the Agriculture skill. The Design Bible already lists a Farming line, Crop Management I-V, marked Not Started; this builds it out in full. Crops grow in city garden plots and livestock in city corrals, producing cooking ingredients, meat, and fiberplast that today come only from harvesting and hunting.

**How it works**

1. **Plant.** Use a garden plot, pick a seed from your inventory, and optionally add fertilizer. Your Crop Management rank sets the highest crop tier you can plant.
2. **Tend.** After a quarter of the grow time, the crop can be tended once, by you or anyone with garden permission. Tending adds 1 to the harvest.
3. **Harvest.** A ripe crop yields 3 items, plus 1 if tended, 1 if fertilized, up to 3 more from Bountiful Harvest, and 1 each from a Greenhouse and Home Ground.
4. **Wither.** A crop left 72 hours after it ripens withers, and its seed is lost.

- Each CD key can have up to 10 crops growing at once, across all its characters and plots.
- A crop belongs to its planter. It counts toward their cap, only they can harvest it, and the harvest uses the perk ranks they hold at that moment. Anyone with garden permission can tend it once and earns the tending XP; a farmhand's tending earns none.
- Harvesting gives Agriculture XP for each item, using the same level-difference formula as fishing and harvesting. Tending gives a smaller amount.
- Garden permission is a new property permission. House owners grant it for their plots, and the mayor for outdoor city plots.
- A planter who can no longer reach their crop, because their permission was removed, the house changed hands, or they left the city, can harvest or clear it from the City Hall clerk, and the harvest goes to their overflow store (/overflow).

**Perks**

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Crop Management I-V | 1, 1, 2, 2, 3 | 0, 10, 20, 30, 40 | Plant and maintain tier 1 to 5 crops | Design Bible, unchanged |
| Bountiful Harvest I-III | 1, 2, 3 | 15, 30, 45 | Harvests yield 1, 2, or 3 more items | New; rank steps follow Treasure Hunter |
| Seed Saver I-III | 1, 2, 2 | 10, 25, 40 | 10%, 20%, or 30% chance a harvest also returns its seed | New |
| Husbandry I-V | 1, 1, 2, 2, 3 | 0, 10, 20, 30, 40 | Raise tier 1 to 5 livestock. Bountiful Harvest also adds to butchering | New; mirrors Crop Management |

- One seed item per crop, 26 in all.

**Livestock**

- A Corral is an outdoor city structure that holds 3 animals and counts against the city's structure limit. A city can have 2 corrals per city level, so 30 animals at most.
- Each CD key can raise up to 6 animals. Young animals are sold by the traveling beast trader and can be resold on the market. Husbandry rank sets the highest tier you can raise.

  - Grow: a young animal becomes an adult 72 hours after it is placed if it has been fed at least 3 times by then, or otherwise at its third feeding.
  - Collect: once every 20 hours from an adult fed in the last 24 hours: 1 manure for Fertilizer, plus 1 milk from Thune.
  - Butcher: adults only. Ends the animal and yields 4 of its meat plus Bountiful Harvest, and 1 of its second product where the table lists one.
  - Crate: packs the animal into an item to move, sell, or trade. A crated animal is paused, with no hunger, growth, or products. Every timer and any penalty it carries resume where they stopped when it is placed again, so crating can't clear a penalty. It doesn't count toward the 6 while crated, since it produces nothing, and placing it needs the Husbandry tier and room under the 6.
- Collecting and butchering give Agriculture XP by the same formula as crops.
- An owner who can't reach their corral can collect from, butcher, or crate their animals from the City Hall clerk, and what that produces goes to their overflow store (/overflow). If the city is removed, its animals go to their owners' overflow stores, crated.

**Farmhands**

- An Engineering-built droid its owner assigns to one of their houses or corrals. It tends its owner's crops there, which counts as tending but earns no XP, and feeds and collects from its owner's animals.
- It works from its own hopper: the owner loads up to 20 crops of feed, and what it collects waits in the hopper, up to 20 items, until the owner empties it. A full hopper stops collecting, and nothing is lost.
- It keeps its schedule while nobody is around. Whenever its house or corral is next processed (a server start, someone entering, or the owner using it), the droid replays everything it would have done since it was last processed, in time order: tending each crop when it becomes ready, feeding each animal as soon as it may eat while feed lasts, and collecting while the hopper has room. When feed runs out, an animal still wanders off 72 hours after its last meal, and a ripe crop still withers 72 hours after ripening, since farmhands don't harvest.
- Processing twice in a row does nothing new, so entering often neither speeds a droid up nor repeats an action.
- The droid's tier sets the highest crop and animal tier it can handle, like the space revamp's droid staff for holdings.

**Design rule**

- Active harvesting stays the fastest way to get materials. A harvest hour is what one character with full Gathering perks gathers in an hour of active harvesting at a tier, measured by store value from harvest logs before release.
- Budget: everything one CD key's characters get passively, from crops, livestock, woodlots, enzyme cultures, and Civic Exchange crates together, averaged over a week, stays under one harvest hour a day at the same tier. Spread over a day, that is under a twentieth of the active rate, more than an order of magnitude slower.
- A single day can run higher, because ripe crops wait up to 72 hours, crated adults can be saved up, and three crates can be opened at once. Those bursts come out of the same capped weekly output, so they don't raise the average.
- The caps that hold it there count per CD key, not per character: 10 crops and woodlots, 6 animals, 1 vat, and 3 crates a week.
- If the measured harvest hour is below the sustained figures in the table, the base crop yield of 3 comes down first, then the crop cap.
- Not counted here: the space revamp's holdings, station Asteroid Drills, and Planetary Depot deliveries, which the space revamp budgets on its own (three holdings at their best pay about 90 minutes of combat income a day). This plan adds nothing to holding output, since Industrial Charter only reduces setbacks, and adds the Wild Space Home Ground's 5% to drills. The Depot changes where holding cargo waits, not how much there is.

| Source, per CD key | Sustained daily average | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crops, tier 4 or 5 | 100 items | 10 crops ripening in 24 h, 10 items each: 3, +1 tended, +1 fertilized, +3 Bountiful Harvest, +1 Greenhouse, +1 Home Ground |
| Crops, tier 1 | 200 items | The same at 12 h, picked twice a day |
| Woodlots | 13 logs | 10 saplings, 4 logs every 3 days, sharing the crop cap |
| Livestock raised for meat | 14 meat, plus up to 2 second products | 6 animals, each butchered 72 hours after placement for 7 meat; eats 12 crops a day and needs a new young animal each time |
| Livestock kept as adults | 7.2 manure, and up to 7.2 milk | 6 adults, each collected every 20 hours |
| Enzyme cultures | 0.5 enzymes | 1 vat per CD key, 2 days per enzyme |
| Civic Exchange crates | 0.43 crates | 3 a week, each worth under 20 minutes of harvesting |

The rows share caps and feed each other, so a CD key gets one crop row and one livestock row, not all of them.

**Farming for other crafts**

Farming feeds most crafts, not only cooking, so other crafters have a reason to buy from farmers or farm themselves.

| Craft | What farming supplies | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smithery, Fabrication, Engineering | Fiberplast from Fiber Shrubs, and wood from Woodlots | Fiberplast appears in about 380 Smithery recipe components, 120 in Fabrication, and 50 in Engineering. Wood is a staple of Fabrication and Smithery. |
| Beast Mastery | Enzyme cultures, plus crops and meat for pet food | Breeders can make the exact enzyme colors a mutation needs instead of hunting for them. The 60 pet food recipes already use crops and meat. |
| Cooking | Every crop, meat, and milk | The original reason for farming. |
| Espionage | Herbs and meat | A few Espionage recipes already use them. |

- **Woodlots:** a sapling planted in an outdoor city plot grows for 3 days and yields 4 logs of its tier: wood, fine wood, ancient wood, aracia wood, or hyphae wood, matching the five harvesting tree tiers. Saplings drop rarely from harvesting trees, Crop Management gates their tier, and each sapling counts toward the 10-crop cap.
- **Enzyme cultures:** a Fermentation Vat, placed in a house in a city, turns 2 crops, 1 manure, and Distilled Water into one enzyme of the breeder's chosen type and color over 2 days. It can make all 15 enzymes (Lyase, Isomerase, and Hydrolase in their five colors each).
  - Cultured enzymes roll like hunted ones, with the same weights, but only from the lowest brackets: primary stats 0.1% to 3.0% (1.1% on average, against 2.1% hunted) and secondary stats 0.1% to 2.0% (0.8%, against 1.6%). A quarter of hunted enzymes roll primary stats above 3%, so hunting stays the source of the best enzymes. A vat only copies colors its owner has found: the first batch of each type and color uses one hunted enzyme of that type and color as its starter, and the vat remembers it. The rarest colors are about 2% of a type's drops, so finding one still takes hunting, and the vat makes it reliable after that. With Breeders' Charter, an average cultured stat gains 0.1% per stage.
  - Cultures are Agriculture recipes at level 20, so farmers can make them for breeders, and each CD key runs one vat at a time.

## Planets

Every planet gives its cities the same bonus, and planets differ in look and story instead of power. Research and incubation speed stay out of planet bonuses, and no bonus exists on only one planet, because lore keeps some players off some planets, such as Jedi on Korriban.

- **Home Ground** (every planet, built once as a City Project): citizens get a separate 5% chance of 1 more item per harvest while on the city's planet, and crops harvested in the city yield 1 more item.
- **Planet identity:** each planet has its own set of decor structures and a signature dish and drink, craftable only at stations in that planet's cities. The food matches the stats of existing dishes of the same tier, and everything can be traded, so a Jedi can still buy Korriban decor.
- **Why location still matters:** planets differ in content level, resources, fishing, and travel links, and Home Port makes a well-placed city a travel hub. Cities are contested through citizenship and elections, not combat.
- Planets with areas open to cities today: Dantooine, Dathomir, Hutlar, Korriban, Mon Cala, Tatooine, and Viscara.

**Planetary councils**

- When a planet-wide story begins, DMs can call that planet's mayors and officers to a council at the planet's main settlement.
- The DM city window (see City Affairs) lists each planet's mayors and officers with their online status and sends the summons as a message and a login notice. The council is posted in each city's Gazette.

## Conflict and PvP

PvP is rare and opt-in, so cities get a place and a reason to fight instead of combat Edicts. Nothing here changes combat numbers.

- **Why the PvP Edicts were cut.** City Law and Militia Muster charged a weekly cost for rules and bonuses that would rarely trigger. A home-turf damage bonus also rewards where a fight happens, not how it is fought.
- **Dueling Ring.** A structure the mayor can place. At the ring, one player challenges another and the target accepts. While the duel lasts, any blow that would kill a duelist subdues them instead, whatever their settings and whoever lands it. Damage from a third player, their pet, or their effect voids the duel, but both duelists stay protected for 6 more seconds, so the blow that voided it still only subdues; after that, normal rules apply. A duelist's own pets and lingering effects count as part of the duel. If both duelists fall at once, both are subdued and the duel is a draw. Leaving the ring or logging out forfeits, a server restart voids the duel with no result, and results go in the Gazette.
- **Tournaments.** The mayor or an officer schedules a tournament at the ring. Entry fees and the prize are held in escrow from the moment it is posted (see City finances). The ring records sign-ups and match results, and every player sees the announcement at login, so visitors come to watch or compete. A duelist who doesn't show within 5 minutes of being called forfeits that match, a server restart replays the match in progress, and the prize is paid when the final ends. A player who interrupts a match is barred from the ring until the tournament ends, and a match interrupted three times is decided by the official running the tournament, with the Gazette saying so.
- **City bounties.** The mayor or an officer can post a bounty on an identity that shows the PvP flag in the Player List: a character, or a disguise, which keeps its own flags. The bounty names only that identity, so it never reveals who is behind a disguise. The reward goes into escrow, the target is told privately, and the bounty is public in the Gazette. An identity can be targeted only after its player accepts a one-time notice that cities can post bounties on it; identities already flagged at launch see the notice at their next login and can't be targeted until they accept. Any PvP damage dealt or taken starts a 60-second combat window. During it, the player can't turn the flag off or change identity, and a request to do either waits until the window ends, so nobody escapes a fight or gives away a disguise mid-fight. Whoever the game credits with the target's defeat or subdual collects the bounty: a pet's or droid's kill counts for its owner, damage over time for whoever applied it, and a bounty still pays if both fall at once. Bounties pay anywhere PvP is already possible, never in a duel. Outside a combat window, a target showing another identity or with the flag off can't be claimed, and the 7-day expiry keeps running. Each identity can be claimed at most once a week; counting by character instead would let hunters link a disguise to its owner.
- **Rivalry without combat.** Embargo, Alliance, and City Affairs give cities ways to feud that never need a fight.
- **No fighting over city land.** Contested Sites were cut after player review. Most cities sit where an NPC power rules in lore, so routine battles over them would break the setting and need DMs to sort out. Taking or raiding a settlement stays roleplay that DMs oversee. The space revamp's Wild Space, where PvP deaths drop ship modules, is where resources are fought over, and a warzone area built for contested territory is a separate suggestion for mappers and staff.

## Planetary industry

Once the space revamp's trade and holdings work ships, each city becomes the industry hub for its planet. This release ships after the space revamp, so that work is already live.

- **Starport trade tariff.** This is already in the space revamp plan: a city Starport's Trade Exchange charges a mayor-set tariff paid into the treasury. Cities get income beyond citizen taxes.
- **Planetary Depot.** The space revamp already sells holdings at a city's Starport, lets owners collect and deliver there, and moves a holding to the NPC dock if its Starport is lost. The Depot adds one thing: a citizen can move an existing holding, bought at the NPC dock, to the city Starport and back, once every 7 days. It changes where output waits, not how much. Unlocked at Starport level 2.
- **Industrial Charter.** Citizens' holdings on the city's planet roll setbacks half as often and need half the repair goods (see the Edict catalog). It doesn't raise output: three holdings at their best already reach the space revamp's daily income target of about 90 minutes of combat income, and 10% more would pass it. Bonuses that build up while nobody is watching, such as this one and the Wild Space drill bonus, count only for the time they were active: the game settles a holding's or drill's output up to the moment an Edict, a citizenship, or Home Ground changes, then continues under the new state.
- **Holding events become City Affairs.** A bandit raid or customs inspection at a citizen's holding can raise a situation for the city, such as sending help or paying the fine from the treasury.
- **Stations.** Station Starports get the Planetary Depot for holdings on their orbit's planet. Everything else about stations is in the Stations section.

## Edge cases

The default for anything unclear: Edicts keep running while upkeep is paid, and nothing pushes a struggling city further down.

**Elections and Edicts**

- **Incumbent kept by default** (no candidates or no votes): current Edicts continue, and the incumbent's record is unchanged.
- **Tie:** the incumbent stays with the current Edicts, even if they were one of the tied candidates, since nobody won outright.
- **Edicts and disrepair:** Edict costs count as upkeep. A bill still unpaid at the next weekly check puts the city in disrepair and pauses every Edict until it is paid. The upkeep notice lists Edict costs separately, so the mayor knows what to repeal.
- **Alliance:** a pending Alliance takes a slot but costs nothing until both mayors enact it. It ends if either city repeals it or is destroyed. A city cannot hold an Alliance and an Embargo against the same city.
- **City hopping:** Edict benefits start 7 days after a player becomes a citizen.
- **Vote stuffing:** only characters that have been citizens since before registration opened can vote, and leaving the city drops a vote already cast.
- **Owed taxes:** citizens who owe taxes get no Edict benefits but can still vote.
- **Elections running at launch:** one already in Voting finishes under today's rules, and its winner fills Edict slots from Manage City. One still in Registration lets candidates add a platform.
- **New mayor with a lower City Management rank:** at the next weekly upkeep check the city drops to the level their rank supports, as it does today, and Edicts above the new level pause (see When the city level changes). The city climbs back one level per weekly check once the mayor trains the perk and upkeep is paid.
- Breeders' Charter repealed or suspended: the bonus is added when a stage starts, so a stage already running keeps it.
- **Downtime:** every timer runs in real time. Anything due while the server is down, such as a vote closing, a crop ripening, a lock ending, or a situation expiring, resolves at the next server start.

**City Affairs**

- **New mayor:** when a different player takes the seat, officer appointments and city permissions granted under the old mayor end, and the new mayor grants their own. Open situations stay open.
- **Officer leaves the city:** revoking citizenship ends their office.
- **Nobody handles situations:** they expire with their ignored outcome. No new situations are drawn while the city is in disrepair, so an abandoned city does not spiral.
- **Treasury too low for a choice:** that choice is disabled.
- **Exposed secrets:** when a Scandal exposes a bribe, the Gazette names the official who took it.

**Living cities**

- **Host city lost or Lodge removed mid-week:** the traveler moves to the public hub until next week.
- **Townsfolk, staff, and vendors** cannot be harmed, so PvP and stray area effects never empty a city.
- **Spawns that quests depend on:** they move to the nearest reachable spot outside the buffer and Settled zone within 40 m, and otherwise stay where they are, so no quest breaks when a city is founded nearby.
- Townsfolk or critters switched off: they leave at once and stop appearing until switched back on. Predator situations are unaffected, since an official has to call the hunt.

**Conflict**

- **Duel interrupted:** a third player's attack voids the duel, both duelists stay protected for 6 more seconds, and in a tournament the attacker is barred from the ring.
- **Bounty farming:** the claimant cannot share a CD key with the target or be in the target's party, and each identity can have at most one city bounty claimed per week.
- **Bounty on a citizen:** allowed. A city can hunt its own outlaws.
- **Unclaimed bounty or cancelled tournament:** the reward or prize returns from escrow to the treasury, and entry fees return to the entrants.

**Recall, homes, and planets**

- **Recall stops early:** a recall that gets a candidate and a vote ends the Abandoned path, and the city carries on.
- **House type changed mid-craft:** a craft already open keeps the bonus it started with.
- **House owner leaves the city:** the house keeps its type, plots, and bonuses, since it stands in the city.
- **City lost:** garden plots and their crops go with the city's structures, as all structures do today. Animals go to their owners' overflow stores, crated, and farmhands go back to their owners.
- **Two cities on one planet:** each builds its own Home Ground and gets the same bonus.

**Farming**

- **Plot picked up with a crop in it:** the seed returns to the planter's inventory, and the growth is lost.
- **Planter loses garden permission:** they can still harvest or clear their own crop, at the plot or from the City Hall clerk, which sends the harvest to their overflow store, but cannot plant there again.
- **Planter offline:** crops keep growing in real time, and the 72-hour wither clock still runs.
- **Farming perks refunded:** crops already planted keep growing. The harvest uses the Bountiful Harvest and Seed Saver ranks the planter holds when they harvest.

**Acting mayor**

- **Mayor returns early:** they end the Acting Mayor term from Manage City at any time.
- **Mayor steps down with no officers or Acting Mayor:** they must name an Acting Mayor first, who accepts before the snap election opens.

**Livestock**

- **Corral with animals in it:** it can't be picked up until the owners crate their animals. If an owner left the city more than 7 days ago, the mayor can send their animals, crated, to the owner's overflow store.
- **Farmhand runs out of feed:** it skips feeding, and the 72-hour wander-off clock runs as normal.
- **Owner loses access to the corral:** they can still collect from, butcher, or crate their own animals, at the corral or from the City Hall clerk, which sends what it produces to their overflow store, but cannot add new ones.

**Stations**

- **Station lost:** its structures, plots, stalls, and vats go with it, as on a planet. The space revamp already sends a station building's assigned droid back to its owner's overflow store; farmhands use the same return, and animals go to their owners' overflow stores, crated.
- **Citizen offline when a deck unloads:** the space revamp's rule applies, and the player is saved at the home port.
- **Moving between a station and a planet city:** the same 7-day Edict benefit delay applies, since it is one citizenship.

### Data and services

Edicts are an enum plus a definition list stored on the city record. City processing runs only at module load (`Property.OnModuleLoad`), so every scheduled change lands at a server start, and `ProcessCityLevel` and `ProcessUpkeep` run only when the weekly upkeep date has passed (`ProcessCities`).

**Data**

- `EdictType` enum in its own file. Values are permanent, since an enum rename needs a data migration.
- `EdictDefinition` builder list in `Feature/EdictDefinition/`, like perk and layout definitions: name, description, pillar, group, weekly cost, minimum city level, and whether it names a target city.
- `WorldProperty.Edicts`: `EdictType` to `CityEdict` (state Pending or Active, enacted time, lock end, target city id, from-platform flag, and the single-Edict pause reasons: CityLevel, and Situation with its expiry).
- `WorldProperty.MayorRecord`: the sitting mayor's promised and broken counts, reset when the seat changes hands.
- `Election`: kind (Regular, Recall, SnapConfidence, SnapStepDown), per-candidate Platforms (Edicts, targets, statement), `RegistrationClosesUtc` and `VotingClosesUtc`, and a confidence-vote flag. The ballot freezes when registration closes. Voters qualify from `CitizenSinceUtc` at or before registration opened, so no separate snapshot is stored, and votes from characters that are no longer citizens are dropped at the count. Stored stage dates stop the shorter calendar from moving the cutoff of an election already running.
- `Player.CitizenSinceUtc` for the 7-day benefit delay and voter eligibility, reset when a character rejoins.
- `CityGazetteEntry`: city id (indexed), time, entry type, text, official id, secret flag, hook flag. Keep the newest 200 per city.
- `VoterRewardLedger`: CD key to last reward time. A vote pays only if 28 days have passed, and the entry is written with the first vote, so changing or withdrawing a vote never pays again.
- `WorldProperty.RestrictedTreasury` and `Escrow`: each escrow entry (bounty, tournament, or project id) stores its restricted and unrestricted portions and, for tournaments, each entrant's fee. Projects take restricted funds first; bounties and prizes take only `Treasury`. Every transition (post, pay out, refund, cancel, city deletion) moves each portion back to the balance it came from, in one `DB.Set` of the city record so a restart can't split it. Upkeep and Edicts spend restricted funds first; the withdrawal in `ManageCityViewModel` reads `Treasury` only. Situation gains are tracked over a rolling 7 days against the 20,000 cap.
- City-wide conditions on `WorldProperty`: `NoConfidenceSinceUtc` and `NeglectSinceUtc` (null when clear); Disrepair is derived from `Upkeep > 0` with a `DisrepairDestruction` date, as today. They exist even when the city has no Edicts, so situation drawing and enactment read them directly. Also `NeglectCount`, `RecallStage` (None, First, Second), and the interim holder and step-down acceptance deadline.

**Services**

- Edict service: `IsActive(cityId, type)` and `GetCitizenEdict(player, type)`. An Edict is in effect only when no city-wide condition holds and it has no single-Edict reason. A citizen Edict also needs the player to be a citizen with no taxes owed and 7 days of citizenship. Lookups follow `Property.GetEffectiveUpgradeLevel`. Enacting is refused while a city-wide condition holds. Mid-term enactment charges cost x days to the next check / 7 from the treasury at once; an Edict that resumes between checks adds the same part-week to `Upkeep`.
- `Property.GetCityIdForArea(area)`: walks the instance, building, and city chain that `ElectionViewModel` already walks.
- `Property.ProcessCityElections`: implements the Plan's outcome tables row by row and never opens a snap election from a snap election. Only an outright win (a Yes, or the most votes) enacts a platform over locks and clears `NoConfidenceSinceUtc`; ties never enact. Any election ending with a candidate and a vote clears `NeglectSinceUtc`. A ballot emptied by departures ends as a no-vote election. For an empty second recall it counts citizens whose `Player.LastLoginDate` is on or after 14 days before the recall ended.
- `Property.ProcessUpkeep`: adds a full week for each Edict in effect or paused only by a Situation reason, and itemizes Edicts in the upkeep notice. `PayUpkeep` clears Disrepair at once, and the Edicts it resumes add their part-week through the Edict service.
- `Property.ProcessCityLevel` (weekly check only): after setting the level, add CityLevel reasons to Edicts above their minimum level first, then the newest above the slot count; end the newest officer appointments above the limit; close the newest stalls above the limit. Clear them when the level returns.
- Login notices on `OnModuleEnter` for open election stages, results, and traveler arrivals.
- `Gazette` service: writes entries, posts them to the property Discord webhook, and posts hook entries to a new DM webhook setting beside `PropertyBroadcastWebhookUrl`.
- **Acting Mayor:** an acting-mayor player id and end date on the city. Acting Mayor and interim holder checks allow City Affairs, taxes, upkeep, projects, and enacting into empty slots, and refuse withdrawals, repeals, appointments, and granting permissions. Stepping down records the named interim holder and waits up to 7 days for them to accept; the departed mayor stays `OwnerPlayerId` until the snap election ends.
- **Permissions:** `TransferPermissions` (`Property.cs:1609`) today moves only the mayor's record and leaves every other player's city permissions in place. This release clears other players' city permissions and officers when a different player takes the seat. `ManageEdicts` appears in the permission window, but its grant right (`GrantPermissions[ManageEdicts]`) is held only by the mayor and can't be given, so only the mayor delegates it. `HandleCityAffairs` is not in the permission window; the Officers tab sets it.

### City Affairs

- `SituationType` enum and a `SituationDefinition` builder list in `Feature/SituationDefinition/`: text, choices, requirements (city level, planet, facilities, active Edicts, a skill rank per choice), effects, follow-ups, the ignored outcome, and secret and hook flags. ### Conflict

No combat formula changes. Duels and bounties hook the existing death and subdual path.

- **Dueling Ring:** a new City-only structure with a ring terminal and a trigger radius. Duel state (challenger, target, ring, state, `ProtectedUntil`) lives in memory, so a restart voids it. Order on every damage event to a duelist, before `Death` runs: if the source (or its master, or the creator of a damaging effect) is not a duelist, mark the duel Interrupted and set `ProtectedUntil` to now + 6 s for both duelists, then let the damage land. `Death` takes the subdual path while the duel is Active or `ProtectedUntil` is in the future, so the voiding blow still subdues. A duelist's pets and effects they applied during the duel count as duel damage. Both falling in the same death pass is a draw with both subdued. A disconnect or leaving the radius forfeits. The result goes to the Gazette.
- **Tournaments:** `CityTournament` entity (city, ring, schedule, entry fee, entrants, match results, interruption count per match, and barred players). Fees and the prize sit in `Escrow`. A no-show forfeits after 5 minutes, a restart replays the current match, an interrupter is barred from the ring until the tournament ends, and a third interruption hands the match to the running official. Cancellation refunds fees to entrants and the prize to its balance. On city deletion, fees become pending credits paid at each entrant's next login. All players get a login notice while sign-ups are open.
- **City bounties:** `CityBounty` entity (city, target identity as player id plus disguise id or none, reward in `Escrow`, poster, expiry). Requested and effective state are separate: `PlayerList.``S``etFlag` and `Disguise.Activate`/`Deactivate` change immediately today, so during a PvP combat window (`PvPCombatUntilUtc` on the player, set to now + 60 s on any PvP damage dealt or taken) a PvP flag-off or identity change is queued and applied when the window ends. A claim checks the identity shown at the defeat: it pays if that identity's flag is on, or the window is open. Consent: a `BountyNoticeAcceptedUtc` on the player and on each disguise, unset for everyone at launch, so identities flagged before launch can't be targeted until accepted. Weekly claim limit per identity. Claims run in `Death` on a defeat or subdual, credit a pet's or droid's master and a damaging effect's creator, and reject a killer who shares the target's CD key or party.
